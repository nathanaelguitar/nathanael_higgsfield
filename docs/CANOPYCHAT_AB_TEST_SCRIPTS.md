# CanopyChat face vs. faceless hook tests

These five pairs keep the underlying idea constant while changing only the
messenger. The founder versions use direct eye contact and a phone gesture;
the faceless versions use screen recordings, environmental B-roll, and the
same spoken copy or on-screen text.

Use the same duration, voice, music, captions, and edit template within each
pair. The faceless cut should not imply that CanopyChat can search the public
web or provide legal/compliance guarantees unless that capability has been
verified in the product.

## 1. The hidden toggle

Founder:

> Have you ever opened an AI privacy setting and realized the important toggle
> is buried at the bottom? I don't want a better opt-out. I want the
> conversation to stay on my phone in the first place. That's why I use
> CanopyChat.

Faceless:

- Open on a fast scroll through a generic AI privacy-settings screen.
- Freeze on the bottom-of-page toggle; caption: `WHY IS THIS BURIED?`
- Cut to a phone in local/offline mode and the CanopyChat app.
- Voiceover: `I don't want a better opt-out. I want the conversation to stay on
  my phone in the first place. That's why I use CanopyChat.`

## 2. The water bill

Founder:

> Humans survived millennia without data centers. Nobody survives without clean
> water. I'm not quitting AI. I'm moving it onto my phone, so every question
> doesn't have to become another remote request. That's CanopyChat.

Faceless:

- Hard cut from cooling towers or server-room footage to a running faucet.
- Display the line: `AI DOESN'T FLOAT IN THE CLOUD.`
- Show the same prompt answered in airplane mode on a phone.
- Voiceover: `I'm not quitting AI. I'm moving it onto my phone, so every
  question doesn't have to become another remote request.`

## 3. The privacy audit

Founder:

> People are uploading selfies to cloud AI to find where they appear online.
> That's impressive—and honestly, kind of terrifying. CanopyChat doesn't need
> to scan the internet to help me think. It runs locally, so this conversation
> stays on my device.

Faceless:

- Show a blurred, clearly labeled demonstration of a cloud-AI search interface.
- Cut before displaying any real person's private information.
- Show a phone with network access disabled and CanopyChat open.
- Voiceover: `That's impressive—and honestly, kind of terrifying. CanopyChat
  runs locally, so this conversation stays on my device.`

## 4. The hum

Founder:

> That server noise? That's what AI sounds like when it lives somewhere else. I
> want AI that doesn't send every question away just to answer me. CanopyChat
> runs quietly on my phone.

Faceless:

- Start on a black screen with a low industrial hum and a waveform.
- Reveal night footage of cooling equipment, then cut the audio abruptly.
- Show a silent phone answering a prompt offline.
- Caption: `AI THAT RUNS HERE.`

## 5. The school angle

Founder:

> Teachers shouldn't have to choose between useful AI and student privacy. Before
> a school rolls out a cloud chatbot, ask where the questions go. CanopyChat
> runs on the device, so local conversations don't need to leave the phone.
> That's the kind of AI schools should be evaluating.

Faceless:

- Show a classroom, a student typing, and a generic cloud-routing animation.
- Cut the cloud line and replace it with a phone-local loop.
- Caption: `ASK WHERE THE QUESTIONS GO.`
- Voiceover: `CanopyChat runs on the device, so local conversations don't need to
  leave the phone.`

## Testing order

Run the founder and faceless versions as matched pairs:

1. Hidden toggle — privacy threat and clear visual proof.
2. Water bill — emotional contradiction and environmental stakes.
3. Privacy audit — curiosity and personal relevance.
4. Hum — sensory atmosphere and local infrastructure.
5. School — niche audience and institutional trust.

Use the affective-region predictor on both the spoken audio and the animatic.
Keep the final decision tied to completion, saves, shares, and comments once
the posts have real platform outcomes.
